---
title: "Family, Work, and Growing as a Developer"
tags: [work-life, growth, career]
---

For a long time, I lived away from my family and, in many ways, lived almost like a single person. As I got used to living alone, I realized that outside of work, there wasn’t much else that demanded my attention.

When I was single before getting married, I actually spent more time socializing and meeting different people. After getting married, that naturally changed. I also found myself enjoying spending money on my children more than spending it on myself.

While I was living alone, I was able to dedicate a lot of time to my work and personal growth. But after being reunited with my family, things became very different. It became much harder to focus on my work, and unexpected situations seemed to come up all the time.

Having a young child makes things even more unpredictable. Kids suddenly get sick, unexpected situations arise, and plans can change in an instant. These are things I never really imagined before having children.

Today, for example, I was supposed to go to work, but I ended up arriving almost three hours late. I should have contacted my company in advance to let them know what was happening. But I had to leave immediately to take my child to the hospital, and at one point I was in a situation where I couldn’t even use my phone.

When you have a family, people often talk about work-life balance. But lately, I’ve been thinking about a slightly different question.

> How can I create an environment where I can continue to grow as a developer and stay focused on my work while also being there for my family?

Of course, getting married and having a family changes many things compared to being single. There are more responsibilities, and I can no longer spend all of my time and energy on myself.

But having children—especially young children—feels like a completely different level of challenge. No matter how carefully I plan my time or manage my schedule, something unexpected can happen at any moment.

So these days, I’m thinking less about how to achieve a perfect work-life balance and more about this:

> How can I continue to grow as a developer and stay focused on my work while taking care of my family?

I’m still trying to figure that out.
