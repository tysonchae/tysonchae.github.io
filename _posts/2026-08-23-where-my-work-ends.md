---
title: "Where My Work Ends"
tags: [ownership, team-culture]
---


 A project doesn't stop being your responsibility just because your part is done.

 A teammate once went on vacation just before a release. The developer who stayed behind had to piece together the changes and prepare the build.

 Most of the code wasn't his.

 He was uncomfortable being the one to release it, so we went through it together.

 He didn't need help understanding the code. What he needed was someone to share the uncertainty with.

 That made me think about ownership.

 I used to think ownership meant owning the part I built.

 I still do. But I think it also means caring about what happens after my part is done.

 If I've touched a project, even just a little, I should leave it in a state where someone else can pick it up without having to reconstruct the whole story.

 That might mean writing down some context. Explaining a decision. Pointing out a sharp edge. Or simply being available when someone else has to make the call.

 Not because I need to own everything.

 Quite the opposite.

 Good ownership should make it easier for someone else to take over.

 So these days, I try to think about it simply:

 *If I touched it, I own it.*

 Not forever. Not every decision.

>  Just enough to make sure the next person doesn't inherit a mystery.
