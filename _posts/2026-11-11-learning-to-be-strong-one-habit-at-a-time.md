---
title: "Learning to Be Strong, One Habit at a Time"
tags: [work-life, growth, career]
---

Compared to when I was a young developer in my twenties, what has changed? I’m not talking about a broader perspective or sharper skills. I’m talking about my body, and most of the changes aren’t welcome ones.

My attention span has gotten shorter, and my stamina has noticeably dropped. A few rounds of overtime and I feel like my body is about to fall apart. Back then, I could pull several all-nighters in a row and be fine. Ten years went by in the blink of an eye, and somewhere along the way, without my noticing, I got older and my body started to wear down.

Productivity aside, it’s time I started taking care of my body itself. I live with fairly severe back pain, 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, and I manage it every single day just to keep it from getting worse. And that’s not all. Even without a cold, my condition will suddenly crash, and some days my whole body aches like it’s about to shatter. Sometimes my body loses its ability to regulate temperature, and I get unbearably cold. I’m careful with my body, but the sick days still come.

I’m not the only one. My teammates take sick days from time to time. What’s interesting is that managers take far fewer sick days than individual contributors. I imagine it’s because they carry the responsibility and stress of feeling like they can’t get sick, and that even if they do, they still have to work.

But there’s one teammate I’ve never once seen sick. Not a single day. I’ve never even heard him say he’s feeling a bit off. Of course, some people are just born tough, and others less so. But he doesn’t strike me as naturally sturdy. He seems more like someone who manages his body thoroughly and does it well.

> When I see someone who excels at something I know I need, I should learn from them. So I watched him closely. He manages both the length and quality of his sleep very well. He exercises consistently and keeps a lean body. He also keeps the quantity and quality of his meals steady. Some might see him as slight, but to me, his is a well-maintained body. That’s a remarkable skill.

I need to build my own motivation for exercise and healthy habits, and soon. And in 2027, I want to build a body as strong as his.
