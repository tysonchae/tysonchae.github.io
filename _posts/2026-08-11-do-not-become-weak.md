---
title: "Don’t Become Weak"
tags: [mindset, growth, retro]
---


A decade ago, I had to make some changes and commit code to a repository owned by another team.

While we were working on the project, I got along really well with the dev responsible for that repository. We would joke around, laugh, and have casual conversations. We had a genuinely good relationship.

One day, however, one of the QAs on my team quietly came over to me and asked,

“Do you know when he(the dev res ponsible for that repo) will finish their changes?”

I told him I wasn’t sure.

He then said, “The manager keeps asking me about it. Can you come with me and help me find out?”

So we went over to that person together.

We asked, “The manager was asking about the timeline. Do you have a rough idea of how much longer it might take?”

Unexpectedly, he suddenly raised his voice and started playing the blame game.

He said he wasn’t the bottleneck. He said the problem was that we hadn’t finished our work yet, and that we were the reason he couldn’t move forward.

I was completely caught off guard.

*What happened to him all of a sudden?*

Just moments ago, we had been laughing and talking like friends. But suddenly, the entire atmosphere had changed.

We eventually calmed him down and went back to our desks. We continued working on our own tasks.

Later, he asked us to squash our commits. I said sure.

But then he went straight to our manager and started blaming other people for the fact that his work was falling behind. (which was not true)

As soon as his productivity started to suffer, he began looking for someone else to blame.

Watching him, I felt something I couldn't quite shake.

The moment he felt that his own situation was becoming difficult, he started putting other people down and doing whatever he could to protect himself.

To me, there was something deeply unattractive about that behavior.

At the time, another developer who was working with me on the project watched what was happening and said something I have never forgotten:

> “Don’t become a weak person. You need to have the strength to believe in yourself.”

> “When things go wrong for you, don’t become the kind of weak person who blames others.”

Those words made me realize something.

Maybe being an excellent developer isn’t just about being good at writing code.

Maybe a truly great developer is also someone who has a philosophy of their own.

Most people are happy to share credit and celebrate together when things are going well.

But when things start going badly, some people do the opposite.

Instead of taking responsibility, they try to push the blame onto the people around them. They look for someone else’s mistake so they can protect themselves from criticism.

Of course, things don’t always go according to plan.

Sometimes you can work hard and still fail. Sometimes unexpected problems arise. Sometimes, honestly, you can just be unlucky.

But I believe that those are the moments when you need to believe in yourself even more.

Believe in your ability.

Believe in your strength.

And when things get difficult, have the courage to own your part without putting others down.

That experience, along with that one piece of advice from the developer I was working with, had a much bigger impact on me than I realized at the time.

Ever since then, whenever I find myself in a difficult situation, I try to ask myself:

*“Am I blaming someone else just to protect myself?”*

And another question:

*“Do I believe in myself, even when the situation around me is difficult?”*

Looking back, that brief experience and a single piece of advice changed the way I work and the way I treat people.

More importantly, they became a turning point that helped me grow, not just as a developer, but as a person.
