---
title: "Becoming a Better Developer
tags: [team, culture, retro]
---

There was a time when I truly believed I was becoming a pretty solid developer.

Then one morning, **an early deployment failed.**

I rushed into the office before 6:00 AM, dug through the logs, and became convinced that I had found the root cause. The infrastructure team listened to my explanation and agreed—it all seemed perfectly reasonable.

As I sat back at my desk, **I felt a quiet sense of pride.**

Then an architect from another team arrived early.

I explained what I had found, but instead of simply agreeing, he took a different approach and asked if we could go through the logs together.

**That was when it hit me.**

What I had identified as the root cause was **nothing more than a superficial symptom.**

To make matters worse, when the architect on my own team arrived shortly afterward, he came to exactly the same conclusion.

That morning, for the first time in my career, **I seriously wanted to quit the industry altogether.**

I was standing in front of a massive wall that seemed impossible to climb, finally confronting the limits of my own abilities.

But life went on.

**And so did I.**

I stayed.

Years have passed since that morning, and today, I’m skilled enough to diagnose and resolve a deployment failure like that **almost instantly.**

Looking back, I realize something.

> **The wall I saw that day wasn’t a wall I could never climb. It was simply a wall I wasn’t ready to climb yet.**

So don’t rush yourself by constantly looking at people who are far ahead of you.

You don’t need to burn yourself out trying to catch up with someone else’s journey.

Instead, focus on becoming **just a little better than you were yesterday.**

That small, steady progress is what allows us to keep moving forward on this long journey **without running out of steam.**
